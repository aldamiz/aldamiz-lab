Learning 3 · afibcases.com

How much of a new site's traffic is human?

Status Answered, counter fixedOpened 2026-11-04Next check —

Thesis

The visitor panel said 15 people had read something in a week. On a site that is days old, most of that could be machines dressed as browsers.

Defined process

  • The counter logs every page request on the server, without IP addresses or cookies, so it also sees crawlers that never run JavaScript.
  • Each session was checked against three signals: two pages in the same second, claiming to arrive from inside the site without having entered it, and several different browsers appearing within ten seconds of each other.
  • Only a visitor who opens at least one piece counts as a person; touching the home page and leaving does not.

Partial results

  • Of the 15 "people", 9 were one machine that changed its browser signature on every request and claimed to come from inside the site, between 01:03 and 02:45 on 2 November.
  • After the fix: 8 people since 28 October 2026, 6 of them arriving from Google on the evening of 3 November.
  • By 7 November: 56 sessions disguised as browsers and 81 that only touched the home page.

What we learn so far

On a new site, most of what calls itself a browser is a machine. Counting visits without these filters overstates readers several times over.
